Everyone looks at a critical file upload or RCE flaw and assumes it's an instant win. "Oh, it's PHP? Just drop a webshell and you're root." People treat every bug like it's Log4Shell, expecting a single string injection to instantly phone home and hand over the keys to the kingdom.

If only it were that simple.

You find the vulnerable endpoint and on paper, it's a solid 9.8. But the second you actually try turning that execution into a stable shell, reality sets in:

- **The Extension Game:** You spend an hour cycling through `.php5`, `.phtml`, and `.phar`, fighting MIME type checks and magic byte validations, just to get a file past the upload filter.
- **The `disable_functions` Wall:** You finally get your script on the box, trigger it, and realize `system()`, `exec()`, `passthru()`, and `shell_exec()` are all locked down in `php.ini`. Your critical RCE is suddenly reduced to a glorified file reader.
- **Egress Silence:** Even if you find a way around the function blocks, the target network blocks outbound traffic. Your netcat listener sits there in total silence while you hunt for an open port.
- **The Permission Prison:** You finally get a shell back, but you're trapped running as `www-data`, completely walled in by `open_basedir` with zero write permissions anywhere useful.

By the time an actual stable shell pops, you've burned hours debugging HTTP 500 errors and writing custom payloads. High severity scores look great on paper, but unlike a clean Log4Shell drop, PHP exploitation is an absolute headache in practice.
